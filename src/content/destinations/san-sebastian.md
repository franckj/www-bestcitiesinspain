San Sebastián, Donostia in Basque, sits on the Bay of Biscay about 20 km from the French border. It has 188,487 people (2024) and three city beaches: La Concha, Ondarreta and Zurriola ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n)).

## Is San Sebastián worth visiting?

Yes, for most first-time visitors who like food. The Old Town (Parte Vieja) is packed with pintxo bars, and it opens straight onto the harbour and La Concha bay. Everything below is walkable.

It suits couples, food lovers and anyone combining Spain with south-west France. It suits less well if you want a long list of major museums, or guaranteed sun. Annual rainfall at the Igeldo weather station averages 1,507 mm, with rain on about 141 days a year ([AEMET, 1981–2010 normals](https://www.aemet.es/es/serviciosclimaticos/datosclimatologicos/valoresclimatologicos?l=1024E&k=pva)).

## What to see

- **La Concha bay and promenade.** The beach is 1,500 metres of sand, and the white Concha railing along the promenade is one of the city's symbols. You can swim out towards Santa Clara island all year ([San Sebastián Turismo](https://www.sansebastianturismoa.eus/en/to-do/what-not-to-miss/the-concha-bay)).
- **The Old Town (Parte Vieja).** The city's historic core was walled until 1863 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n)). This is where most pintxo bars are. The pintxos tours on this page start here.
- **Basilica of Santa María del Coro.** A Baroque church completed in 1774, at the end of Nagusia street in the Old Town ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Mary_of_the_Chorus)).
- **Monte Urgull.** The hill above the Old Town rises to 123 m. Mota Castle sits on top, with a history museum run by San Telmo, and a 12 m statue of Christ was added in 1950 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Urgull)).
- **San Telmo Museoa.** The museum of Basque society, in a 16th-century Dominican convent at the foot of Urgull, with a 2011 extension ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Telmo_Museoa)). Tickets €10, reduced €4, free on Tuesdays and for under-25s. Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00–20:00 from April to October and 10:00–19:00 from November to March ([San Telmo Museoa](https://www.santelmomuseoa.eus/index.php?option=com_flexicontent&view=items&id=5217&cid=1&Itemid=50&lang=en), checked 14 October 2026).
- **Monte Igueldo funicular.** The wooden funicular has run for more than 100 years. A return ticket costs €5.50 for adults and €2.50 for children up to 7, and includes entry to the hilltop grounds; a single is €4.00 ([Monte Igueldo](https://www.monteigueldo.es/organiza-tu-visita), checked 14 October 2026). It isn't wheelchair-accessible.
- **Peine del Viento (Comb of the Wind).** Three iron sculptures by Eduardo Chillida, installed in 1977 on the rocks at the end of Ondarreta beach, below Monte Igueldo. The architect Luis Peña Ganchegui designed the granite terraces around them ([Tourism Euskadi](https://turismo.euskadi.eus/en/cultural-heritage/peine-del-viento-the-comb-of-the-wind/webtur00-content/es)).
- **Gros and Zurriola beach.** Across the river from the Old Town, Gros is built on former sandy ground. The Kursaal congress centre overlooks Zurriola beach ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n)).

## How many days

Two to three days. Day one: the Old Town, Santa María del Coro, Monte Urgull and San Telmo, then pintxos in the evening. Day two: walk La Concha to Ondarreta and the Peine del Viento, then take the funicular up Monte Igueldo. A third day leaves room for a cider house, the coast to Getaria, or a day trip to [Bilbao](/destinations/bilbao/).

## Getting there

| From | How | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Madrid Chamartín | Renfe Alvia or Intercity, 3 direct a day | 5 h 25 to 5 h 37 (Renfe timetable, 21 Oct 2026) |
| Barcelona Sants | Renfe Alvia, 1 direct a day | About 6 h 15 min to 6 h 45 min |
| Bilbao | Alsa coach, more than 12 a day | About 1 h 20 min |

Train times come from published timetables, not from Renfe directly: Madrid, Bilbao by train and Hendaye from [Seat61](https://www.seat61.com/international-trains/trains-from-San-Sebastian.htm), Barcelona from [Everyrail](https://everyrail.com/trains/barcelona-to-san-sebastian/). Coach details are from [Alsa](https://www.alsa.com/pt/autocarro/bilbau-san-sebastian). Check [renfe.com](https://www.renfe.com/) before booking.

**Airports.** San Sebastián airport is in Hondarribia, about 22 km from the city. Buses E20, E21, E23, E27 and E28 run between the two, and a single costs €2.90 ([Spain.info](https://www.spain.info/en/aeroplane/san-sebastian-airport/)). Bilbao airport also has direct long-distance coaches to San Sebastián ([Spain.info](https://www.spain.info/en/aeroplane/bilbao-airport/)). See [getting around Spain](/getting-around-spain/).

## Where to stay

- **Old Town (Parte Vieja):** for pintxo bars on your doorstep and a short walk to La Concha and Monte Urgull.
- **Gros:** across the river, next to Zurriola beach. Close to the Old Town without being in the middle of the bars.

## When to go

San Sebastián has a mild, wet oceanic climate. At the Igeldo weather station, average highs range from 11.0 °C in January to 22.5 °C in August. November is the wettest month (169 mm) and July the driest (86 mm) ([AEMET, 1981–2010](https://www.aemet.es/es/serviciosclimaticos/datosclimatologicos/valoresclimatologicos?l=1024E&k=pva)). The station sits on Monte Igueldo, at 250 m.

June to September gives the best beach weather. The San Sebastián International Film Festival takes place every September; the 2026 edition ran from 18 to 26 September ([festival rules](https://www.sansebastianfestival.com/2026/film_registration/rules_and_regulations/1/23002/in)). Book rooms early for those dates. See [the best time to visit Spain](/best-time-to-visit-spain/).
