## Is Valencia worth visiting?

Yes, for most first-time visitors. Valencia is Spain's third-largest city, with 841,558 people ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia)). It has a Gothic old town, a park that runs through the city in an old riverbed, and the futuristic City of Arts and Sciences at the end of that park.

It suits you if you like food markets and want a slower pace than Madrid or Barcelona. [Paella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paella) comes from the Valencian Community, and its modern form is traced to the rice-growing area around the Albufera lagoon next to the city.

You might skip it if your trip is short and you want the big-name art and architecture: Madrid has the Prado, Barcelona has Gaudí. Valencia's top sights are good, but none is on that level of fame.

## What to see

- **Silk Exchange (Lonja de la Seda):** a late Gothic trading hall built from 1483, on the UNESCO World Heritage list since 1996 ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/782/)). Entry is €2, and free on Sundays and public holidays. Open Monday to Saturday 10:00–19:00, Sundays and holidays 10:00–14:00 ([Visit València](https://www.visitvalencia.com/que-hacer-valencia/cultura-valenciana/monumentos-en-valencia/lonja-seda), checked 14 October 2026).
- **Valencia Cathedral:** consecrated in 1238, mostly Valencian Gothic. One chapel holds a cup that many believe is the Holy Grail ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Cathedral)). The visit costs €12 with an audio guide in 10 languages, including English. From October to June it's open Monday to Friday 10:30–18:30, Saturday 10:30–17:30 and Sunday 14:00–17:30; from 1 July to 30 September, Saturdays run to 18:30 and Sundays 14:00–18:30 ([Catedral de Valencia](https://catedraldevalencia.es/visita-cultural/horarios-y-tarifas/), checked 14 October 2026).
- **Central Market (Mercado Central):** a food market under a large dome, right across from the Silk Exchange. Open Monday to Saturday, 7:30–15:00 ([Mercado Central](https://www.mercadocentralvalencia.es/), checked 14 October 2026). Go in the morning.
- **Torres de Serranos:** a 14th-century city gate, built between 1392 and 1398. It's one of the few parts of the old city wall still standing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torres_de_Serranos)).
- **Turia Garden:** a public park in the former bed of the Turia, which was diverted after the 1957 flood. It's about 8.5 km long and opened in 1986 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turia_Garden)). Walk or cycle it from the old town to the City of Arts and Sciences.
- **City of Arts and Sciences:** Santiago Calatrava and Félix Candela's complex, opened in stages from 1998 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Arts_and_Sciences)). You can walk around the buildings for free.
- **Oceanogràfic:** the aquarium in the same complex. An adult general ticket costs €35.90 to €43.05 depending on the date. Hours change through the year, from 10:00–16:00 up to 10:00–22:00, so check the calendar before you go ([Oceanogràfic](https://www.oceanografic.org/en/planifica-tu-visita-al-oceanografic-de-valencia/prices-and-schedules/), checked 14 October 2026).
- **Albufera:** a freshwater lagoon and nature park 11 km south of the city, surrounded by rice fields ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albufera_de_Val%C3%A8ncia)). Go for a boat trip at sunset and a paella in El Palmar.

## How many days

**2 days** covers the old town on day one (cathedral, Silk Exchange, Central Market, Torres de Serranos) and the Turia Garden and City of Arts and Sciences on day two. **3 days** lets you add the Oceanogràfic or a half day at the Albufera. See our [itineraries](/itineraries/) to fit Valencia into a longer trip.

## Getting there

| From | Train | Time | Direct trains |
|---|---|---|---|
| Madrid (mostly Chamartín) | Renfe AVE / AVLO | 1 h 54 min – 2 h 05 min | 15 |
| Barcelona Sants | Renfe Euromed | 2 h 47 min – 3 h 26 min | 6 |

Times and counts are from [Renfe's open timetable data](https://data.renfe.com/dataset/horarios-de-alta-velocidad-larga-distancia-y-media-distancia) for Wednesday 21 October 2026 (downloaded 14 October 2026). Trains from Madrid and Barcelona arrive at València-Joaquín Sorolla. On the Barcelona route, one Intercity (3 h 27 min) and one Alvia (3 h 57 min) also run direct. [Ouigo](https://www.ouigo.com/es/en/destinations) and [Iryo](https://iryo.eu/en/destinations/train-to-madrid) also run trains between Madrid and Valencia. More in [getting around Spain](/getting-around-spain/).

**By air:** Valencia Airport is 8 km west of the city. Metrovalencia lines 3 and 5 run from the airport to the city centre in about 15 minutes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Airport)).

## Where to stay

- **Ciutat Vella (the old town):** you can walk to the cathedral, the Silk Exchange and the Central Market, and the Turia Garden runs along its northern edge.
- **Russafa:** just south of the old town and the Estació del Nord. Pick it if you'd rather sleep outside the busiest streets of the centre.

More options in [where to stay in Spain](/where-to-stay/).

## When to go

Valencia has a mild climate. The average high is 16.4 °C in January and 30.2 °C in August. October is the wettest month, with an average of 77 mm of rain ([AEMET normals for Valencia, 1981–2010](https://www.aemet.es/es/serviciosclimaticos/datosclimatologicos/valoresclimatologicos?l=8416&k=val)). Spring and autumn suit walking the city best. See [the best time to visit Spain](/best-time-to-visit-spain/).

The big event is **Fallas**, with its main days from 15 to 19 March. UNESCO added it to its list of intangible cultural heritage in 2016 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falles)). Book your room early if you plan to go then.
