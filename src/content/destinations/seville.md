## Is Seville worth visiting?

Yes, for most first-time visitors to Spain. Seville is the capital of Andalusia, with about 688,700 people ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville)). Its three main monuments, the **Cathedral, the Real Alcázar and the Archivo de Indias**, were listed together as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1987 ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/383/)).

It suits you if you like architecture, long evenings outdoors and a centre you can cross on foot. It suits you less if you're travelling in July or August and don't cope with heat, or if you want beaches: Seville is inland, on the Guadalquivir river.

## What to see

- **Real Alcázar.** The royal palace, built between the Christian conquest of 1248 and the 16th century with strong Moorish influence ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/383/)). General entry **€15.50**, reduced **€8** (over-65s, students aged 14–30, European Youth Card holders). Free with a ticket for under-13s with an adult and for people born or living in Seville. The upper royal rooms (Cuarto Real Alto) cost **€5.50** extra. Open 09:30–17:00 from 1 October to 31 March and 09:30–19:00 from 1 April to 30 September. The ticket office in the Patio de Banderas opens at 09:00 and takes cards only ([Real Alcázar](https://alcazarsevilla.org/prepara-la-visita/), checked 14 October 2026).
- **Cathedral and the Giralda.** UNESCO calls the cathedral the largest Gothic building in Europe; it holds the tomb of Christopher Columbus, and the Giralda bell tower is the former minaret of the Almohad mosque ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/383/)). Tickets are **€13 online or €14 at the ticket office** (reduced €7/€8) and include the Giralda and the Church of El Salvador. Open Monday to Saturday 10:45–19:00, Sunday 14:30–19:00; the ticket office closes at 18:00. There's a free visit on Sundays (not holidays) 16:30–18:00 with an online booking and limited places ([Seville Cathedral](https://www.catedraldesevilla.es/en/cultural-visit/schedules-and-rates/), checked 14 October 2026).
- **Plaza de España.** Built by Aníbal González for the 1929 Ibero-American Exposition, in María Luisa Park. Its tiled alcoves each represent a Spanish province. Free to walk around ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_de_Espa%C3%B1a_(Seville))).
- **Barrio de Santa Cruz.** The former Jewish quarter of the medieval city, between the Alcázar and Calle Santa María la Blanca. The Cathedral and the Giralda sit at its edge ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz,_Seville)).
- **Triana.** The district across the river, reached by the Puente de Isabel II. It has a long pottery and tile trade and a strong flamenco tradition. The Mercado de Triana stands by the bridge, with the foundations of the Castillo de San Jorge, a former seat of the Inquisition, in its basement ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triana,_Seville)).
- **Las Setas (Metropol Parasol).** A wooden canopy with a rooftop walkway, the Mirador 360º. The general ticket is **from €16**. Open 09:30–01:00, last entry 00:15 ([Setas de Sevilla](https://setasdesevilla.com/experiencia-general), checked 14 October 2026).
- **Casa de Pilatos.** A noble palace run by the Medinaceli Foundation. Ground floor **€12**, upper floor **€6**; open daily 09:00–18:00; children up to 11 free with an adult ([Fundación Medinaceli](https://www.fundacionmedinaceli.org/monumentos/pilatos/), checked 14 October 2026).

## How many days

**2–3 days.** One day for the Cathedral, the Giralda and the Real Alcázar, which sit next to each other. One day for Plaza de España, Triana and Las Setas. A third day for Casa de Pilatos, a flamenco show or a day trip to Córdoba. For combining Seville with Granada and Madrid, see our [itineraries](/itineraries/).

## Getting there

| From | How | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Madrid | High-speed train (Renfe AVE, Iryo, Ouigo) | 2 h 38 to 3 h 09 (Renfe timetable, 21 Oct 2026) |
| Barcelona | High-speed train | From 5 h 18 min |
| Granada | Renfe Avant, direct | About 2 h 40 min (estimate) |

Madrid times come from Renfe's published timetable for 21 October 2026 (see [getting around Spain](/getting-around-spain/)); the Barcelona time comes from [Spain.info](https://www.spain.info/en/route/explore-spain-on-high-speed-trains/), Spain's official tourism site. Ouigo gives an average of 2 h 47 min from Madrid Atocha to Sevilla Santa Justa, with fares from €9 ([Ouigo](https://www.ouigo.com/es/tren-tren-madrid-sevilla-barato)). Iryo has run Madrid–Córdoba–Seville since March 2023 ([Hosteltur](https://www.hosteltur.com/156774_iryo-estrena-conexion-de-alta-velocidad-en-la-ruta-madrid-cordoba-sevilla.html)). The Granada time is a timetable estimate from booking sites, not an official figure; check [Renfe](https://www.renfe.com/) for your date. All trains arrive at **Sevilla Santa Justa**.

**By air:** Seville Airport (SVQ) is 10 km east of the centre. An airport bus links it with the Plaza de Armas bus station via Santa Justa in about 40 minutes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville_Airport)).

## Where to stay

- **Santa Cruz and around the Cathedral:** you can walk to the Alcázar, the Cathedral and most of the old town. It's the main tourist area, so expect crowds by day.
- **Triana:** across the river over the Puente de Isabel II, with its own market and a local feel, a short walk from the old town.

Compare areas in our [where to stay in Spain](/where-to-stay/) guide.

## When to go

Spring and autumn. Average daily highs at Seville Airport (AEMET, 1991–2020) are **16.3 °C in January, 24.0 °C in April, 36.3 °C in July and 26.3 °C in October** ([Wikipedia, from AEMET data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville)). The **Feria de Abril** runs for a week in spring, on a fairground with 1,052 casetas ([Visita Sevilla](https://visitasevilla.es/en/the-april-fair/)).
